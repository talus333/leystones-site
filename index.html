Having been there counts

Nothing in Leystones opens from your couch. That is the point: what you read, you earned by going.

1

Go there

The map starts under a soft fog. It clears as you walk, and stones nearby show as pale markers waiting to be reached.

2

Read what was left

Stand at a stone and it opens: the history of the mill beside you, what grows here, a story a neighbour told. Every fact carries its source.

3

Leave your own

Set a stone for everyone, for your friends, for one person, or just for yourself. Add a photo, a voice note, even a riddle to open it.

Four kinds of stone

Each has its own shape on the map, so you know what is waiting before you get there. Switch any of them off when you only want one kind of day.

History

Standing stones. What happened here, who built it, and the legends people still tell.

Nature

Cairns. The land now and long ago: bedrock, birds, plants and seasons.

Adventure

Waymarks. Trails, put-ins, lookouts and swimming spots, with safety notes.

Personal

Lantern stones. A memory kept in a place, for you or for someone you choose.

Leave a message where it happened

Share with friends by leaving messages at places. They unlock when your friend gets there, and not before.

A note on the bench where you used to meet. A voice message at the top of the hill. A riddle only they could answer. You get a ping when they find it.

Your whereabouts stay yours

An app about places has to be careful with them. These are built in, not settings you have to find.

  • Your home is off the map. Mark a home zone and nothing is recorded or shared inside it. It never leaves your phone.
  • Lookups use a rough area. When the app asks what wildlife or heritage is nearby, it sends an area about 10 km across, not where you stand.
  • You choose who reads a stone. Everyone, your friends, one friend, or only you.
  • No adverts. Your information is never sold.
